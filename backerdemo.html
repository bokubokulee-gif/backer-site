First principles

Two things are undeniably true.

01

Attention is the scarcest resource on the internet.

Views already become currency. Human attention prices ads, sponsorships, distribution, cultural leverage, and founder reach. It has already become an asset.

02

The attention economy is broken.

Only the platforms and the creators make money. There is no economic upside for the consumers — the viewers who already spend money and create the value the whole system runs on.

The original sin of the creator economy

There is no aligned upside for the people who matter most — the fans.

Tipping already proves fans will fund creators.

Twitch, Douyin, Bilibili, YouTube — every platform has a money flow from fans to creators. Look at who captures the value.

The question was never whether fans will fund creators. They already do. The question is: what if funding came with upside?

The infrastructure problem

You can't invest in a market where the metrics can't be trusted.

Every number on a creator's profile is potentially fabricated. Followers, views, likes, comments — all purchasable. An entire shadow industry exists to inflate them, and platforms have little incentive to stop it.

Are these 4,000 followers real humans or purchased bots?

Did this video get 80K views organically, or bought overnight?

Is engagement genuine, or manufactured by coordinated rings?

Are these fans sticky — do they come back — or one-time exposures?

Without answers, investment isn't investment. It's a lottery on unverifiable data.

Proof of Attention — an open protocol

Platforms manufacture attention.
We verify it.

Every number on a creator's profile can be manufactured — fabricated, even. So we learn from the best market makers in the world: quantitative hedge funds monitor the sentiment around a stock by analyzing news channels, analyst reports on Substack and Medium, Reddit, and Twitter. Backer applies the same discipline to attention.

Our own PoA — Proof of Attention — algorithms. Agents monitor and analyze the publicly available data on every major platform: likes, comments, reposts, views; the frequency of their spikes; the sentiment inside the comments. The output is a whole picture of the composition of attention — per content, per creator.
