Attention simulation / Method

A population is a joint distribution.

To simulate a decision, represent who can encounter it, how relevant traits occur together, and what changes when the conditions change.

Backer Research20 September 2026Research note · 01

Research protocol · Interactive examples use assumed parameters. Population fit and predictive performance remain to be evaluated.

01

Specify the world before sampling people.

A simulation needs a population, a time interval, a set of available actions and a condition being varied. “How will customers respond?” is incomplete. “What share of eligible customers starts a trial within seven days under each onboarding condition?” defines a denominator, an outcome and a horizon.

A person’s state may include interests, prior exposure, resources and existing commitments. Those traits form a joint distribution p(x). Marginal totals are constraints on that distribution, not a full description of it. Correlations matter whenever an action requires several conditions at once.

02

Separate population fit from response fit.

The population model specifies which states occur together. The response model specifies how behavior varies with state and scenario z. Aggregation combines the two:

P(Y = a | z) = ∫ P(Y = a | x, z) p(x) dxThe target population p(x) is held fixed in a paired comparison. If an intervention also changes population composition, that process must be modeled explicitly.

A plausible profile is not evidence for either factor. Park and colleagues evaluate agents grounded in interviews and structured self-reports on held-out individual responses. Kolluri and colleagues study behavioral fine-tuning across social-science experiments. These supply task-specific evidence for grounding and response learning, not a universal population simulator.

03

Compare scenarios under an explicit mechanism.

For a paired run, preserve the initial population and non-intervened conditions. Change the nominated parameter, then propagate the state transitions. With stochastic simulation, shared random draws can reduce variance in the estimated difference; they do not remove bias in the mechanism.

Δ(z, z₀) = E[Y(z)] − E[Y(z₀)]A model contrast becomes a causal estimate only under an identified intervention and defensible assumptions linking the model to the real setting.

Backer’s separate product demonstration uses independent teaching figures for finite attention, conditional choice and distributional error. Its use-case results are authored examples. The town preview visualizes fictional agents. Neither should be described as drawing a representative sample of real people or learning parameters during the displayed run.

04

Validate at more than one resolution.

First test population coverage and supported joint relationships. Then test conditional behavior on evidence excluded from model development. Finally compare aggregate distributions and, when identified intervention evidence exists, the direction and magnitude of changes.

TVD(P, Q) = ½ Σₐ |P(a) − Q(a)|For matching, exhaustive action categories, total variation distance ranges from 0 to 1. A small aggregate error can coexist with large errors in particular cohorts.

Simile’s confidence article compares simulated and observed action distributions and evaluates a separate error predictor. For Backer, these are design requirements for future evaluation. Changing an assumption and observing a different output shows that a mechanism runs, not that its response is empirically correct.

05

Report the limits with the result.

Report the population boundary, source coverage, scenario assumptions and evaluation domain with each result. Distinguish variability across sampled agents from uncertainty about parameters and uncertainty about the model itself.

If a recommendation reverses under plausible alternative assumptions, report that sensitivity. More synthetic agents can reduce Monte Carlo noise while leaving the same structural error intact. A larger population is not, by itself, stronger validation.
