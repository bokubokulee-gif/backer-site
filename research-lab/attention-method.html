Human attention prediction / Method

Measure attention before predicting it.

A forecast needs an observable target, a fixed horizon and a denominator. “Attention” becomes scientific only after those choices are explicit.

Backer Research20 September 2026Research note · 01

Research protocol · The preview uses illustrative data. Live platform feeds and prospective forecast scores are not connected.

01

Define the denominator.

For an instrumented population, one possible target is a topic’s share of observed attention time during the next interval. Let dik be eligible viewing seconds person i assigns to topic k. Then a topic share is:

sₖ = Σᵢ dᵢₖ / Σᵢ Σⱼ dᵢⱼInclude an “other” category and a mutually exclusive allocation rule. The denominator is observed eligible time, not all human attention.

Public posts, likes, searches and viewing counts are different measurements. Without person-level exposure and dwell data, a system may forecast a share of those observed events; it should name that proxy. A normalized search index cannot silently become a percentage of people.

02

Distinguish a signal from a state.

A feed records encounters and actions. Sustained interest is a state inferred from their sequence. A minimal candidate state accumulates exposure and decays when exposure stops:

mₜ = ρmₜ₋₁ + (1 − ρ)eₜ, 0 ≤ ρ < 1m is a bounded illustrative state and e is normalized exposure. A fitted model would have to estimate retention and test whether this state predicts a declared outcome.

The memory–retrieval–action architecture in Generative Agents motivates separating experience from subsequent behavior. Its demonstrations concern believable agents; they do not establish that this equation forecasts human attention. Here the recurrence is a deliberately inspectable hypothesis.

03

Build the record without knowing the future.

Every signal needs an event timestamp, collection timestamp, source, unit and coverage boundary. Resolve duplicated links and cross-posts before measuring diffusion. Fix the topic taxonomy using only information available at the forecast origin; redefining an emerging topic after it succeeds leaks the outcome into the target.

Separate observed zero from unavailable data. A missing feed is not evidence that attention disappeared. When coverage changes, report the measured population and source set alongside the estimate rather than drawing a continuous curve across incompatible denominators.

04

Score the trajectory and the decision.

Use rolling forecast origins and untouched future windows. Compare against persistence, recent momentum and a seasonal baseline. Report error by topic age, platform coverage and forecast horizon; keep related topics or source events together when constructing held-out tests.

For topic-share vectors, report total variation distance with sampling uncertainty. For a prespecified event such as “share exceeds a threshold by tomorrow,” use a proper probability score and reliability analysis. For onset time, evaluate timing error and missed events. These targets require different metrics. Proper scoring rules reward honest probabilistic forecasts; a vivid path does not supply uncertainty by itself.

05

Validate the confidence signal separately.

A narrow simulated distribution can still be wrong. Repeatedly sampling the same assumptions measures simulation variability, not missing data or model misspecification. Simile’s confidence work treats expected simulation error as a distinct prediction problem evaluated on held-out evidence.

For Backer, the equivalent requirement is prospective: declare when a forecast is expected to fail, then check that claim against realized error. Until such evidence exists, scenario ranges should remain sensitivity ranges rather than calibrated confidence intervals.
