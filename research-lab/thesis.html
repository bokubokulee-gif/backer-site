Trading behavior / Thesis

Attention changes the opportunity set.

Capital can follow attention through discovery and participation. Whether it improves prices depends on who arrives, what they know, and what the market can absorb.

Backer Research20 September 2026Research note · 01

Research thesis · These propositions remain to be tested against observed behavior.

01

Before a trade, something must become visible.

Investors do not inspect every available contract or asset. Attention restricts the set from which they choose. Barber and Odean document attention-related buying among individual investors in equities. This supplies a mechanism worth testing; it does not establish that attention predicts returns, or that the same effect transfers unchanged to event markets.

Backer’s proposed research target is this selection process. A new narrative may bring previously inactive participants into a market, change which evidence they encounter, or concentrate them around the same interpretation. Each route can increase activity without increasing information.

02

A price is an equilibrium observation.

A quote aggregates willingness to trade under budgets, inventory, rules and competing orders. It is not a survey average of everyone’s beliefs. An informative participant with little capital may have less price impact than a less informed participant with a larger order.

Diercks, Katz and Wright explicitly distinguish risk-neutral from physical probabilities when studying Kalshi’s macroeconomic contracts. That distinction matters whenever risk compensation or thin trading affects inferred distributions. Treating every quoted cent as a literal belief would erase the phenomenon a behavioral model is meant to explain.

Central proposition

Attention helps explain which information reaches a market. Execution and aggregation determine how that information enters prices.

03

Three tests that could reject the thesis.

Discovery. Conditional on prior activity and event characteristics, a change in independent exposure should improve prediction of subsequent participation. If exposure adds no held-out information beyond existing price and volume signals, the attention layer is redundant for that target.

Composition. Equal increases in attention can produce different action distributions when they reach different cohorts. Compare like-for-like event windows and preserve pre-event cohort definitions. If one aggregate attention count predicts as well as the cohort model, added population detail has not earned its complexity.

Market capacity. The relationship between participation and price movement should vary with spread and depth. If that interaction does not replicate in held-out events, an attention-to-price mechanism needs revision. None of these observational comparisons alone identifies a causal effect of exposure.

04

More participation is a hypothesis about information.

Kalshi’s calibration working paper reports associations between participation and forecast quality. Its observational design does not establish that adding participants causes better calibration. Participation can rise because an event is easier to resolve, more newsworthy, or already better understood.

A stronger Backer study would separate exposure to a signal from the signal’s informational content. A randomized distribution experiment, or a defensible natural experiment, could estimate whether additional exposure changes participation and whether the resulting forecasts improve. The latter is a separate outcome.

05

Follow capital without equating it with value.

Market turnover records exchanges between counterparties; it is not the same as net capital inflow. Price changes can revalue existing positions without comparable new spending. A complete measurement account therefore keeps attention, order flow, net positions and valuation separate.

The thesis is useful if it improves a declared forecast or decision after these distinctions survive measurement. A visually persuasive crowd is insufficient. The research must show when population behavior adds information beyond the market’s own observable state.
