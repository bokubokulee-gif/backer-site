Human attention prediction / Thesis

Attention is a state, not a count.

A moment of visibility can become memory, repeated exposure, or a costly decision. The transition between those states is the research problem.

Backer Research20 September 2026Research note · 01

Research thesis · The preview illustrates mechanisms whose predictive value remains to be tested.

01

A spike does not reveal what persists.

Two topics can receive the same number of views and leave different traces. One is encountered incidentally; another is sought out, revisited and discussed across otherwise separate communities. The counts match while the sequence and population differ.

Backer’s hypothesis is that the history of exposure and the structure of its audience help predict the next distribution of attention. The useful target is a future observable—return visits, topic share or a specified action—not an undefined score called cultural importance.

02

The opportunity cost is part of the mechanism.

Time spent on one object is unavailable to another within the same attention budget. An increase in a topic’s share may reflect its own growth, the decline of competing topics, or a change in total observed activity. A model should retain both the numerator and denominator so that these explanations remain distinguishable.

Exposure also changes the person who receives it: familiarity, remembered claims and expectations can affect the next encounter. That is a reason to study stateful models. It is not permission to infer private beliefs from a public click.

Generative Agents demonstrates an explicit memory architecture. Research on agents grounded in self-reports supplies a different lesson: representations need evidence about the people they purport to simulate. Neither result transfers automatically to open-internet attention prediction.

03

Three separable propositions.

Persistence. A history of repeat attention should predict future return more accurately than current reach alone. Test by matching topics on current exposure and evaluating the subsequent interval. Failure against a simple recency baseline would weaken the case for a richer memory model.

Transmission. Exposure crossing community boundaries may change who returns, rather than merely adding more views. Test whether known network structure improves held-out diffusion forecasts after controlling for initial reach. A bridge can transmit rejection as well as enthusiasm; its effect has no assumed positive sign.

Conversion. Costly action should depend on attention together with constraints and alternatives. Estimate a separate conditional outcome, with an explicit eligible population. If a single exposure count performs equally well, the proposed decision layer adds no demonstrated value.

04

Capital follows through conditional decisions.

Central proposition

Attention changes what enters consideration. Preferences, constraints and institutions determine what receives resources.

In an internet setting, the relevant transition might be from repeated viewing to a paid membership. In a company, it might be from awareness to trial and a procurement decision. In a financial market, it might be from noticing an event to revising a position.

Evidence of attention-related equity buying makes the financial pathway plausible, but does not make it universal. Attention may also generate criticism, postponement or avoidance. A model that forces every increase in attention into greater spending has encoded its conclusion.

05

What would constitute progress?

A useful system should beat transparent baselines on future data, preserve subgroup differences and identify settings where it lacks coverage. It should also show whether its additional complexity changes a decision under realistic costs.

The strongest result would be a prospectively registered improvement that survives a new time period, source mix or audience. The weakest would be a retrospective narrative that explains every outcome after observing it. Backer’s research direction is toward the former; the current public artifacts remain illustrative.
