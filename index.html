First principles

Two things are undeniably true.

01

Attention is the scarcest resource on the internet.

Every platform — TikTok, YouTube, Spotify, RED — is a marketplace for human attention. Creators produce it, platforms package it, advertisers buy it. The creator economy isn't a media trend. It's the infrastructure of the internet economy.

02

Early believers create compounding value — and capture none of it.

When a creator goes from 800 followers to 800,000, the platform keeps the data and ad revenue, brands pay a premium for access, the creator gains influence. The fans who were there from day one get a shout-out and a badge. They built the asset. They got nothing.

The original sin of the creator economy

There is no aligned upside for the people who matter most — the fans.

Tipping already proves fans will fund creators.

Twitch, Douyin, Bilibili, YouTube — every platform has a money flow from fans to creators. Look at who captures the value.

The infrastructure problem

You can't invest in a market where the metrics can't be trusted.

Every number on a creator's profile is potentially fabricated. Followers, views, likes, comments — all purchasable. An entire shadow industry exists to inflate them, and platforms have little incentive to stop it.

Are these 4,000 followers real humans or purchased bots?

Did this video get 80K views organically, or bought overnight?

Is engagement genuine, or manufactured by coordinated rings?

Are these fans sticky — do they come back — or one-time exposures?

Without answers, investment isn't investment. It's a lottery on unverifiable data.

Proof of Attention — an open protocol

Platforms manufacture attention.
We verify it.

An agentic LLM truth layer that infers audience quality under partial observability. Not bot detection — audience underwriting. Public, auditable, cross-platform, and forkable, because in a market where trust is the only product, the scoring model can't be a black box.

Why open-source is the product, not a detail

Investors need to know the scoring isn't manipulated. Creators need to know it's fair and improvable. An open model is the difference between a black-box credit score and a public financial standard.

The pool balance is the price: side with the crowd and win pennies — be right against it and get paid multiples.

The one-line version: you win the pool minus the fee, in proportion to your share of the winning side. On a perp, your dollar simply tracks the index.

Taking a position on a defined, measurable milestone — "50K subscribers in 12 months" — from as little as $1. It's not a donation, not a tip, and not equity in a person. If the milestone hits, your position pays under the contract terms. If it misses, the stake was the risk you accepted — like any early bet.

Milestone and PK markets are pooled: every stake on both sides forms one pool. At resolution a platform fee comes off the top (proposed 10% — final fees TBD), and the rest is paid to the winning side in proportion to each backer's share of it:

Payout = Stake × Pool × (1 − fee) ÷ Winning side

Worked example: a $100 pool split $20 YES / $80 NO, and you hold $1 on YES. The milestone hits → $90 is paid out, your share is 1/20 of it → $4.50, a +350% return. If it misses, a $1 NO backer collects $90 × 1/80 = $1.125 (+12.5%). Try your own numbers in the calculator above.

From the fee, not from backers' losses. Under the proposed split, 60% of the market fee is Backer's revenue and 40% is paid to the creator as a royalty — independent of the outcome, so a creator earns from the volume their market attracts whether the milestone hits or misses. In a PK battle the two creators share the royalty. All rates are proposals, to be determined.

Because the pool balance is the price. When 80% of the money disagrees with you and you turn out right, you split the pool with a small winning side — that's the 4.5×. When you side with the crowd, you split it with almost everyone — pennies. Early, correct conviction is exactly what the market pays for. That's the same asymmetry that rewards a fan who spots a creator at 800 followers.

A continuous position with no pool and no expiry: your dollar tracks a creator's attention index (built on verified Proof-of-Attention data). Payout = Stake × (index at exit ÷ index at entry), minus a small trade fee charged on entry and exit (proposed ~0.5% each way, TBD). Index up 20% → $1 becomes ≈ $1.19.

Every listed creator carries a Proof-of-Attention read before a dollar is priced — an independent, cross-platform, auditable standard for whether an audience is real and whether it will compound. Think Moody's, not a better spam filter. Suspicious profiles aren't hidden; they're shown with the reason flagged in plain language, and position limits scale down with lower confidence.

It's the same four conditions behind any real market: high attention, genuine uncertainty, measurable outcomes, strong disagreement. The difference from a casino is structural — you get underwriting information (Proof of Attention), transparent pricing (the pool is the odds), position limits tied to verifiability, and a platform that earns on volume rather than on your losses.
