Inside the model

One population.
Four stages of a decision.

Trace attention from the first encounter to the final commitment. Scroll through the sequence or select a stage.Select a stage and explore what changes.

01 / ENCOUNTER

A signal must
reach someone.

Reach determines who can encounter a signal. Closing a channel removes that opportunity for one group.

Launch a signal, close a channel, then follow a profile to inspect what changed.

02 / REMEMBER

Exposure ends.
Memory decays.

A signal fades from memory. Groups differ in how much recall they need to keep the option in consideration.

Play time or send a reminder. A ring marks profiles still considering the option.

03 / CHOOSE

Choice has
a threshold.

Effort, cost and perceived risk separate consideration from action. People can still defer.

Remove a barrier. Select a profile to inspect why it acts or defers.

04 / ALLOCATE

Choices carry
different weight.

Equal numbers of choices need not move equal resources. Capacity determines how much each participant can commit.

Move a reserve parcel to group C. Choices and the total budget stay fixed; committed resources change.

The demonstration follows 100 fixed profiles: 40 in A, 30 in B and 30 in C. Their ranks and response thresholds are assigned deterministically for this illustration. They are not sampled from real people.

Exposure. A profile encounters the signal when its assigned exposure rank is below reach. Memory. Its retained signal is initial strength × 0.86days. Group thresholds of 0.20, 0.35 and 0.50 determine whether the option remains in consideration.

Choice. A considering profile acts when its assigned willingness exceeds friction. All other profiles defer. Allocation. Each acting profile commits its capacity: 1 unit in A, 2 in B, and the assigned capacity in C. Each reserve parcel adds 1 unit to every C profile (30 units total). Moving parcels between C and the reserve preserves a total budget of 400 units. Uncommitted capacity remains available. This is an accounting illustration, with no prices, market clearing or estimated causal effects.

Parameters update all four stages; scrolling preserves them. This transparent demonstration uses assumed rules and does not establish predictive accuracy.

an external test.
